# Logo drafts (for review)

Seven example logos for the team to compare. **These are drafts: the website does
not use them**, except Draft 0, which became the live logo in October 2026.

Drafts 1 to 6 each hold four SVG files:

- `logo.svg`: the full logo (symbol plus the words), for light backgrounds
- `logo-dark.svg`: the same logo, recoloured for navy or other dark backgrounds
- `mark.svg`: the symbol on its own (square), for the browser-tab icon and social avatars
- `mark-dark.svg`: the symbol on its own, for dark backgrounds

The words "Young Medical Engineers" are set in Source Serif 4 (the site's heading
font when the drafts were made) and converted to shapes, so the files look the
same everywhere without the font installed.

Draft 0 is today's logo artwork with one change, so its folder holds PNG and JPG
files instead (see its section below).

None of the drafts uses a red cross. A red cross on a white background is a
protected emblem (Geneva Conventions; in the US, 18 U.S.C. § 706), so the drafts
use a heart, a heartbeat line, hands or a DNA helix as the "care" symbol instead,
or (Draft 0) keep the cross but not in red.

Drafts 2, 5 and 6 contain no heart, for a look that leans more toward engineering.
Drafts 1 to 6 use no rounded rectangles, matching the site's square corners.

## 0. Today's logo with a steel cross (fix)
Today's logo with one change: the outline of the cross is steel `#5F6F8F` instead
of red (light steel `#B9C2D3` in the dark versions). Its shape, thickness and
position are unchanged, and the YME letters, the gear and the words are pixel for
pixel the same as the files the site uses now (the social card is a JPEG, so
re-saving it shifts other pixels by an invisible amount).

Why: US law (18 U.S.C. § 706) reserves "the emblem of the Greek red cross on a white
ground", and anything "colored in imitation thereof", for the American Red Cross
and the armed forces' medical services. A cross in another colour is not that
emblem, and changing the colour is the fix the American Red Cross itself suggests.
(This is a plain summary, not legal advice.)

## 1. YME Heart and Gear (refresh of today's logo)
Keeps the familiar structure: bold YME letters with a half gear behind the E. The
red cross becomes a red heart, and everything is flat (no outlines or gradients).
Colours: brand navy `#1B365D`, red `#C8102E`, steel `#5F6F8F`.

## 2. YME Pulse (refresh of today's logo)
The YME letters with a heartbeat line leading into them: medicine and engineering
in one simple, legible mark. The square symbol is a navy tile (square corners) with
the heartbeat line and a red dot, which stays clear even as a 16-pixel browser icon.
Colours: brand navy and red.

## 3. Heart Gear (new)
One symbol: a heart whose left half is a gear and whose right half is a warm coral,
so care and engineering literally join. Friendly and humanitarian, and it works
without the words.
Colours: navy plus a warm coral accent `#E8705A` (also used for "Medical").

## 4. Helping Hand (new)
Two cupped hands holding a coral heart, on a soft sand badge with a gear-toothed
edge. The most humanitarian of the set; the gear edge keeps the engineering link.
Colours: navy, coral `#E8705A` and sand `#F5EEE4`.

## 5. Bionic Hand (new, no heart)
An open robotic hand: a square palm, four fanned fingers built from square segments,
a two-part thumb and a steel wrist cuff. Red dots mark the finger and thumb joints,
so it reads as a prosthetic hand: engineering that helps people.
Colours: brand navy, red and steel.

## 6. DNA Helix (new, no heart)
A double helix with one navy strand and one red strand that take turns passing in
front, joined by steel rungs. Biology drawn with square ends and engineering
precision.
Colours: brand navy, red and steel.
