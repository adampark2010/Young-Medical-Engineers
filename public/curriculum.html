Tutoring Curriculum

Medical engineering is built on a handful of school subjects: biology, chemistry, physics, math through calculus, and anatomy and physiology. These are the ones our tutors help with, from middle school through AP. Every session starts from your own class and what you are working toward, whether that is a test next week or a subject you want to get ahead in, so use this page as a map of what we can cover, not as a fixed syllabus.

What We Cover

  • Biology

    Middle school

    • Cells and what they do
    • Body systems
    • Heredity and traits

    High school

    • Cell structure and transport
    • Genetics and DNA
    • Evolution and ecology
    • The immune system

    AP Biology

    • Cellular energy
    • Gene expression and regulation
    • Cell communication

    In medical engineering: growing replacement tissue, delivering drugs to the right cells, and tests that read DNA.

  • Chemistry

    Middle school

    • Atoms, elements and compounds
    • Mixtures and solutions
    • Physical and chemical change

    High school

    • The periodic table and bonding
    • Reactions and stoichiometry
    • Gases, acids and bases

    AP Chemistry

    • Equilibrium
    • Kinetics
    • Thermodynamics and electrochemistry

    In medical engineering: materials the body will accept, the contrast agents that make scans clearer, and how medicines are made.

  • Physics

    Middle school

    • Forces and motion
    • Energy
    • Waves, light and sound

    High school

    • Newton’s laws, energy and momentum
    • Waves and optics
    • Electricity and circuits

    AP Physics

    • Physics 1 and 2
    • Physics C: mechanics, electricity and magnetism

    In medical engineering: the loads a prosthetic joint has to carry, how ultrasound and imaging work, and the circuits in pacemakers and sensors.

  • Math, Algebra Through Calculus

    Middle school

    • Pre-algebra
    • Ratios, proportions and equations

    High school

    • Algebra I and II
    • Geometry and trigonometry
    • Precalculus

    AP Calculus

    • Limits and derivatives
    • Integrals and their uses
    • AB and BC

    In medical engineering: modelling how a drug dose rises and fades in the body, reading signals from a heart monitor, and designing devices to exact sizes.

  • Anatomy and Physiology

    High school

    • Skeletal and muscular systems
    • Nervous and cardiovascular systems
    • Respiratory and digestive systems
    • Homeostasis: how the body keeps itself steady
    • Medical vocabulary

    In medical engineering: every device is designed around the body it serves, from heart valves and ventilators to prosthetic hands.

  • AP and Standardized-Test Science

    Exam preparation

    • AP Biology, Chemistry and Physics
    • Free-response questions
    • The ACT Science section

    Skills

    • Reading graphs, tables and experiments
    • Working under time limits
    • Spotting what a question is really asking

    In medical engineering: researchers read data and judge experiments the same way, every day.

A Path Into Medical Engineering

Schools order their courses differently, so check yours with a counselor. This is one common way to build toward the field.

  1. Middle School

    Build the Base

    Get comfortable with pre-algebra and the basics of life, earth and physical science.

  2. 9th and 10th Grade

    Biology and Chemistry

    Take biology and chemistry alongside algebra and geometry.

  3. 11th Grade

    Physics and Trigonometry

    Add physics, finish algebra II and trigonometry, and try an AP science you enjoy.

  4. 12th Grade

    Calculus and the Body

    Take calculus, and anatomy and physiology if your school offers it.
